# Design: Campus Customs storefront

**Concept:** Yale heritage meets a modern fashion editorial. One design system covers all five pages, and every change below is meant to make shoppers stay longer and buy more easily.

## What changed and why

| Area | What we changed | Why it helps people stay and buy |
|---|---|---|
| **Colour** | Deep Yale blue `#00356B`, warm ivory `#F6F0E4`, white and a pale-blue accent, set as tokens in `index.css`. | The site instantly reads as Yale, so it feels like the shop they came for. Consistent frames make 102 different photos look like one curated collection. |
| **Product photography** | Every photo is shown **whole**, centred in its frame (no cropping), on the **same ivory background**. 74 of the 102 supplied photos were on black or had black side bars; the backend makes cleaned copies where black touching the edge, and enclosed black gaps under the arms, become transparent with a smoothed edge, while dark hood linings are kept (`backend/images.py`), and white backgrounds blend into the ivory frame. Only the hover close-up and the product-page zoom crop on purpose. | Shoppers see the entire garment, and the grid reads as one consistent, curated collection instead of a patchwork of black and white photos. |
| **Type** | Oversized Libre Caslon serif headlines (a classic oldstyle serif in the spirit of Yale's own typeface, which is not freely licensed), Inter for body text, and Caveat for handwritten notes. All fonts are self-hosted. | The serif feels like heritage and the sans keeps prices and sizes easy to read. The site makes no outside font requests, so pages load fast. |
| **Hierarchy** | Every section follows the same pattern: a numbered eyebrow ("01 · The edit"), one big serif headline, then one clear action. Prices are set in the serif, larger than surrounding text. | Shoppers always know where they are and what to do next. Price is never hunted for. |
| **Bulldog mascot** | One HTML/CSS bulldog in a varsity "Y" sweatshirt, in five poses: peeking over the hero photo, waving on About, sitting on Log in, holding a pennant on Create account, and a head for the chat avatar and logo. When something is added to the bag, it pops up happy: "Woof! Added to your bag." | A friendly, consistent character gives the brand personality and makes adding to the bag feel rewarding. That small moment of delight encourages the next add. |
| **Creative details** | Stitched (dashed) lines on frames, buttons and dividers. Pennant-shaped labels ("Sold out", "Only 2 left in XL", "27 styles"). Scrapbook captions with tape. A hand-drawn Gothic campus skyline, built in code, in the footer, collage and auth panels. A pennant garland on About. | Embroidery-inspired details echo the product itself. Pennant labels make stock warnings impossible to miss without shouting. |
| **Motion** | Content fades up as you scroll, the hero letters rise in, the category ribbon scrolls slowly, and the bag badge bumps. The detail photo zooms toward the cursor. All motion turns off under "reduce motion". | Motion guides the eye to the next section and confirms actions, but stays restrained and accessible. |
| **Home** | Magazine hero with oversized outline "YALE", the vintage bulldog crewneck in a stitched arch, the peeking bulldog, a taped price caption and "A little Yale. Everywhere." Below: a **Shop the collection** button, an asymmetric category edit with live counts, a "Bulldog-approved" feature with a stamp and real price, four picks, a campus scrapbook collage, and a promise row. | A memorable first screen with one obvious action. Every section is a doorway into a category or a product. |
| **Products** | Editorial header, category chips, a refine bar (size in stock, price, colour swatches, sort) and generous spacing. **Hover shows a close-up of the graphic.** A **Quick view** opens a modal with sizes and **Add to bag** without leaving the grid. | Shoppers compare quickly, see detail without clicking away, and can buy straight from the grid. Fewer clicks means fewer drop-offs. |
| **Product page** | Breadcrumbs, a stitched photo frame with cursor zoom ("hover to see the stitching"), size buttons marked "In stock" or "Out of stock" (sold-out sizes struck through and disabled), and the exact count for a size shown once it is selected ("Size XL: only 2 left"), **Add to bag · $68.00**, size-aware "Ask our assistant", and "Similar styles in stock". | Choosing a size and adding to the bag is one tap each. Shoppers who hit a sold-out size are shown alternatives instead of leaving. |
| **Bag** | A slide-in bag with thumbnails, quantity steppers capped at real stock ("That's all we have in XL"), remove and subtotal. It is saved on the device. Checkout honestly says online checkout isn't live yet and gives the store contacts. | A real bag lets shoppers collect what they want, and the stock cap prevents disappointment later. The honest checkout note keeps trust. |
| **About** | Editorial layout: huge headline with a handwritten aside, numbered chapters with outline numerals, a dotted-leader list of what's on the shelves (live counts), polaroids for who we dress, a promise list, and a visit card over the skyline. It makes no claim of official affiliation and invents no history; a disclaimer says so. | Story builds affection for the brand, and every chapter links back into the shop. |
| **Log in / Create account** | Split screen: a short form on one side, a full-height Yale-blue panel on the other. "Welcome back to the pack." with the sitting varsity bulldog; "Join the pack." with the pennant bulldog. | Accounts feel like joining a club rather than filling in a form, which encourages sign-ups (and saved chat history). |
| **Chat** | "Bulldog Concierge" with a winking bulldog avatar, a dotted ivory background, a stitched header, typing dots, page-aware suggestion chips and an "Ask the bulldog" launcher. | The assistant feels like part of the brand, not a bolted-on widget, so more shoppers use it. |
| **Navigation and mobile** | The nav lists exactly the five standalone pages: Home, Products and About Us on the left, Log In and Create Account on the right (categories live on the Products page, not in the nav). Centred logo with bulldog head, a dashed underline on the active link, and a Bag button with count. On phones: a menu button with the same five pages, a two-column product grid, the auth panel shrunk to a banner, and no sideways scrolling on any page. | Shopping and account actions are always one tap away on any screen. |
| **Accessibility** | Skip link, visible focus rings, blue-on-ivory text at strong contrast, labelled buttons and dialogs (Esc closes quick view and the bag), and reduced-motion support. | Everyone can shop, and a polished, predictable interface builds trust. |

## Checked in the running app

All five pages were screenshotted at 1440 px and 390 px (`output/screenshots/p10-*.png`), with no console errors. Quick view → choose size → Add to bag shows the bulldog toast and the bag count updates. Adding XL three times stops at the 2 in stock. The bag subtotal is correct ($224.00 for $88 + 2 × $68) and survives a reload. The mobile menu opens and closes on navigation. Earlier features (log in, chat results on the page, cards opening product pages) still work.
